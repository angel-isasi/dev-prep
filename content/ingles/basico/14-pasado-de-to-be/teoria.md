## En una frase

**was** y **were** son el pasado de am, is y are. Sirven para decir dónde estabas, cómo te sentías y cómo era algo: I was nervous yesterday → Ayer estaba nervioso.

## Cómo se forma

| Persona | Afirmativa | Negativa | Pregunta |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / he / she / it | was | wasn't | Was he...? |
| you / we / they | were | weren't | Were you...? |

- I was at home last night. → Anoche estuve en casa.
- They weren't at the party. → Ellos no estuvieron en la fiesta.
- Were you nervous? Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't. → ¿Estabas nervioso? Sí. / No.
- How was the exam? It was easy. → ¿Qué tal el examen? Fue fácil.
- There was a problem. There were a lot of people. → Hubo un problema. Había mucha gente.
- I was born in 2005. → Nací en 2005.

Expresiones de tiempo: **yesterday, last night, last week, last year, two days ago, the day before yesterday**.
- **ago** va después del tiempo: three years ago → hace tres años.
- **last** va sin "the": last week → la semana pasada.

-ed o -ing en adjetivos:
- I was **bored** → Estaba aburrido (cómo me sentía).
- The class was **boring** → La clase era aburrida (cómo era).
- Igual con excited / exciting.

## Errores típicos

- I born in Lima ✗ → I was born in Lima ✓
- They was at home ✗ → They were at home ✓
- Ago three years ✗ → Three years ago ✓
- The last week ✗ → Last week ✓ (para decir "la semana pasada").
- I was boring in class ✗ (significa "yo era aburrido") → I was bored in class ✓
- Did you were at home? ✗ → Were you at home? ✓ (was y were arman la pregunta solos).

## Pronunciación

- **was** dentro de la frase suena débil, casi "uz": "I was there" → "ái uz der". En la respuesta corta suena fuerte: "Yes, I was" → "iés, ái uás".
- **were** es una sola sílaba: "uer".
- En adjetivos, la -ed suena "id" solo después de t o d: excited "eksáitid". En los demás no suma sílaba: bored "bórd", worried "uérid".
- **ago** lleva la fuerza al final: "agóu".
